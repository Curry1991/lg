.kriilunaus 07/05/2025 3:05 AM
For LG TVs whose WiFi module is connected internally via a USB2.0 bus
(which is the majority of LG TVs), a maximum bandwidth of around 300
MBit/sec is possible. This also applies to USB to Ethernet GBit adapters
when connected via a USB2.0 port of the TV. The theoretical maximum net
data rate achievable via USB 2.0 with 13 packets per microframe is
~406Mbps. However, practically no USB 2.0 controller is able to use 13
packets, in reality the limit is ~10. Thus, the maximum net data rate
that can be achieved is ~312.5Mbps. If lower data rates are achieved via
the WiFi module than via the 100Mbps Ethernet port, in almost all cases
this is simply due to a poor WiFi connection (wrong channel, wrong
frequency band, etc.) between the TV and your WiFi router. Also note
that the UHD BluRay specification defines a limit of 128Mbps for videos.
In practice, however, the vast majority of all movie titles remain far
below this and do not even reach 100Mbps. So as long as you don't rewind
or fast-forward a video like crazy, you shouldn't have any problems even
with the 100 Mbps Ethernet connection of the TV.
