satdx62 07/21/2024 1:37 PM
On a simple RP2040 the GPIO's work with 3.3 volts, but their LEDs need 5
volts and this can become a problem on the data line. Therefore, you
have to install a level shifter between the data line output of the
RP2040 and the data line of the LEDs. See also connection examples in my
guide. As the Ambilight does not permanently switch the LEDs at full
brightness, a power supply unit with 12 to 14 A output power is also
sufficient. It is important that the +5 volt line to the LED power
supply is protected by a fuse.

Both “Adafruit Feather RP2040 SCORPIO” and “Adafruit ItsyBitsy RP2040”
consist of the same hardware, with the difference that the “Adafruit
Feather RP2040 SCORPIO” has 8 channel NeoPixel drivers and is therefore
interesting for larger projects with multiple LED segments. RP2040
32-bit Cortex M0+ dual-core running at ~125 MHz @ 3.3V logic and power,
264 KB RAM, 8 MB SPI FLASH Chip for storing files and
CircuitPython/MicroPython code storage. As for the refresh rate of the
“Adafruit ItsyBitsy RP2040”, with 1 LED segment with up to 300 LEDs, you
need not worry. See picture. However, I could not perceive any
differences above 60 Hz refresh rate. The transmission speed via the
serial USB interface of both controllers is the same and, thanks to the
HyperSerialPico drivers, faster than all other known solutions. (edited)

USB->TTL —> WT32-ETH01 GND —> GND TX —> RX0 RX —> TX0 5V —> 5V If you
take a closer look: Yes, it is correct that TX is connected to RX0 and
RX to TX0. If the USB->TTL adapter offers a choice between 5V and 3.3
volts, then 5V should be selected. Last but not least, IO0 (next to RX0)
must now be connected to GND for flashing. Note: The labeling of the
ETH0 board in the example image is incorrect. In fact, the IO1
connection is “TX0” and the IO3 connection is “RX0” and not “TXD” and
“RXD” as printed on the connection diagram. (edited)

[] morphrnatus btw For TV's screen cap we need to connect Adafruit via
usb of tv?
mr.meorzipan 06/27/2024 3:37 PM
Screen will be captured with homebrew app named 'Piccap', this app needs
root privileges, therefore you Must root your LG TV. Another app named
'HyperHDR' will connect to the local stream from Piccap. You will setup
HyperHDR with how many leds you have and whatever else and where it
should spit out the processed data for the led strip, in this case the
USB port of the TV. You will connect the rp2040 to the USB port of the
TV, it must be flashed with the correct firmware offered by Awawa, for
that specific rp2040. The rp2040 will then control the led strip. An
rp2040 outputs data through it's gpio pins at 3.3v, but the ledstrip
talks at 5v, if it gets a signal at 3.3v, it would malfunction, this is
why you need a 3.3v > 5v level shifter. You could have either have a
seperate level shifter, the '74AHCT125N', or a built-in level shifter,
like your itsy comes with. Alternatively, if it was a different TV, for
capturing whatever a display is gonna view, HDMI can be captured, with a
HDMI splitter, HDMI USB capture device, a raspberri Pi 3B+(or newer),
and Hyperion/HyperHDR. But Piccap+HyperHDR on LG TV is much more
convenient and low latency, works on all content display shows, even TV
channels, what won't work is DRM content inside WebOS, where Piccap only
sees a black screen, however, this does not apply to a device plugged in
HDMI, where then your ambilight setup can work with DRM content. In your
case with rooted LG TV, you can capture from any HDMI port (whatever the
TV displays) and don't need expensive HDMI 2.1 splitters orbother
hardware. (edited)

[] morphrnatus Fuse is necessary? Doesn't power supply & rp2040 comes
with protection?
mr.meorzipan 06/27/2024 3:08 PM
An additional fuse between the power supply and ledstrip is intended to
protect the led strip against heavy overcurrent damage in case you f up
or some other failure occurs, so the ledsstrip or some other thing
forbsome reason pulls a ungodly amount of current, starting a fire on
the led strip and possibly also burning the cables and killing the PSU
if it had to output for example 100A. Most PSU's also come with a fuse,
but you should mostly use that for protecting the power supply.
Depending on how many how big your TV is, how long your 60/m led strip
needs to travel, power usage changes. Yes, you wouldn't use it at 100%,
wouldn't recommend either since it would be uncomfortably and annoyingly
bright, and significantly decreases lifespan, but it's best to always to
prepare the wiring for 100% usage. (edited)

Also, if you ever have to replace or RMA your TV, if you stick the leds
on the TV, it may be a pain to remove and the glue may not be reusable.
If you, instead, stick it on a thin aluminium frame (or wood, or sturdy
cardboard), it would make reuse of your leds easier, and may be easier
to get an even spread, if the back of a TV is uneven. Additionally, the
back of an OLED is used to cool the OLED, if you stick lights on the
back, and they get hot, it's possible your screen might be more
susceptible to burn-in when you display HDR content, or view a lot of
very bright SDR content. If you use aluminium, the frame can work as
heatsink for the leds and help extend lifespan, as leds live longer the
cooler they stay, the less bright they output, the less strain you put
on them. Uaing a frame you could also try point the leds at an angle of
30° so the light spreads farther, which means you might be able to get
away turning down the brightness of the leds since you don't have to
blast light as much to get same spread. This means slightly less heat,
and less power usage. (edited)

satdx62 06/25/2024 5:50 PM
Hello, you can also use other LED controllers than “Adafruit Feather
Scorpio 2040” for HyperSerialPico with built-in level shifter, for
example, “Pimorini Plasma Stick 2040”, “Pimorini Plasma 2040” or
“Adafruit ItsyBitsy RP2040”. However, if you plan to operate your
Ambilight with more than one segment in the future, the “Adafruit
Feather Scorpio 2040” is the best controller, as it has an 8-channel
output. There is also the HyperSerial ESP8266/ESP32 from awawa-dev.
However, a level shifter must be connected in between, as the logic here
also works with 3.3 volts, as with the RP 2040. Both HyperSerialPico and
HyperSerial from awawa-dev are connected to the LG via USB interface and
thanks to the higher transfer rate, there is no perceptible lag at all.
However, the most common controller is WLED on ESP D1 Mini or ESP32,
which communicates with the TV via Wi-Fi. It can therefore happen that
the Ambilight does not run completely synchronously with the TV picture
in the event of interference or poor Wi-Fi connections. Occasionally
there are controllers based on ESP32 with built-in LAN, where the
transmission remains stable and there is no lag. So there are many
options available. As I have tested all these LED controllers, I would
recommend HyperSerialPico to everyone.

Here you can find a list of recommended RP2040's, and what data pin is
used. https://github.com/awawa-dev/HyperHDR/discussions/561 Generally we
say to get a Pimorino Plasma or Adata Feather Scorpio, cause then you
have the option to do multi-segment led strip wiring, which essentially
means cutting led strip in half and wiring each half to it's own data
pin on the rp2040(instead of a long led strip to 1 data pin), which
allows double fast refresh rates....it mostly matters if using around
300 leds. With an fast enough LG TV and WebOS, if rooted, you can
install and run piccap and HyperHDR on your TV, the output can come from
the TV's USB port. All the hardware you need for you then, is led
controller (with built-in level shifter, or if your led controller
doesn't have one, an external 3.3v > 5v level shifter, a 74AHCT125N, you
can not use a cheap slow one), have the led strip, power supply, and
between the power supply and led strip a fuse (attached between the
power cable) to protect the led strip from overcurrent if you did
something wrong so the led strip draws too much current it and maybe
also the power supply would die, and wires. As for the wires between the
led strip and the power supply, and every corner that you interconnect
led strip behind the TV, you will use copper wires that are thick enough
and capable of passing the amount of current the led would pull from the
power supply. If you don't and the wires are not capable cause they are
too thin and maybe also made of a material that is less conductive, the
wires will heat up and can burn or melt the insulation jacket, the led
strip will malfunction as voltage drops too much, and power supply may
compensate for the loss by pulling more current, which can be bad for
your power supply, in which case the fuse of the power supply might pop
to protect the power supply from dieing. (edited)

mr.meorzipan 06/15/2024 2:15 PM
Please only use the TV's USB port for data, not for power. The main
thing with gaining root privileges and doing ambilight, is Piccap, an
homebrew tool, can then function correctly and record the display, this
can then be offered to another program like HyperHDR or Hyperion through
local or network, which will process the video into something a led
strip could use. If you have configured HyperHDR correctly, telling how
many leds you have and how your leds need to be configured, you can
output that data over the USB port of the TV, or whatever HyperHDR or
Hyperion is running on. You will then use a led controller to use the
output from HyperHDR or Hyperion and control the ledstrip, this will be
a Raspberri RP2040, flashed with Awawa's firmware written specifically
for the specific RP2040 you bought. For example, If you bought a
standard rp2040, or a Adata Feather 2040 Scorpio, or a Pimorino Plasma
2040, you must download the firmware written for that specific rp2040
from Awawa's Github. You will use a RP2040 with a 3.3v > 5v level
shifter, between the data pin of the rp2040 and the led strip, this can
either be a 74AHCT125N chip if you use a standard rp2040, or a built-in
level shifter (if you use a pimorino plasma or a feather scorpio). If
you do not do this, and let the data pin of the rp2040 output it's 3.3v
data to a 5v led strip, your led strip will malfunction (flicker, wrong
colors, not turn on, etc). You will use an appropriate power supply to
power the led strip. For an 5V SK6812 Cold White (note, it's actually
neutral, close to 6500K) of 30 leds per 5 meter (150 leds), you will buy
a power supply that can at least supply 8A at 5v. In actual use, unless
you run the leds at 100% brightness, which is way too bright and a bad
idea since it will significantly shorten lifespan, you will likely use
50% brightness of lower, so power usage is lower, but if your power
supply can not supply at 100% brightness, you might break power supply
or it's fuse.

I believe that when using the ESP32 S2 you do not need a separate data
for the second segment. Multiple data lines mostly apply to RP2040 with
multiple segment output. Please read the awawa-dev instructions
carefully, because, you have to compile this yourself and adapt it
accordingly in the platformio.ini file, under
“SECOND_SEGMENT_START_INDEX” in the HyperSerialESP32 firmware.
https://github.com/awawa-dev/HyperSerialESP32?tab=readme-ov-file#compiling
https://github.com/awawa-dev/HyperSerialESP32?tab=readme-ov-file#multi-segment-wiring
“Divide a long strip of LEDs into 2 smaller, equal parts.
SECOND_SEGMENT_START_INDEX in the HyperSerialESP32 firmware is therefore
the total number of LEDs divided by 2. Build your first segment in the
traditional way, e.g. clockwise, so that it starts somewhere in the
middle of the lower part of the frame/TV and ends in the middle of the
upper part of the frame/TV. Start the second segment in the opposite
direction to the first, e.g. counterclockwise (the
SECOND_SEGMENT_REVERSED option in the HyperSerialESP32 firmware
configuration must be enabled). So it starts somewhere in the middle of
the lower part of the frame/TV and ends in the middle of the upper part
of the TV/frame. Both segments could optionally be connected at the top,
but only 5v and ground (NOT the data line). The data line for both
segments starts somewhere in the middle of the bottom of the TV/frame
(where each of the LED strips starts) The configuration in HyperHDR does
not change! It should be configured as a single, continuous segment.
Everything is done transparently in the HyperSerialESP32 firmware and
has no influence on the configuration of the LED strips in HyperHDR”.

Hi. I myself only know the setups that start in the middle of the bottom
edges and move clockwise, so I don't know if they can start anywhere
else. Only awawa-dev can answer that in GitHub. If you only use 60 LEDs
per meter, and about 300 LEDs, you really don't need a two-segment
solution when watching TV. Your eyes will not be able to perceive the
difference. So you can simply use one segment, which simplifies the
whole setup. Since the GND strip below the GPIO's on the RP2040 Adafruit
Feather Scorpio has the same electrical connection, it doesn't matter
where you take the GND from to the two segment. Ideally, you just
disconnect the DATA line on the stripe for the second segment from where
it should start, so that the +5 volts and GND runs continuously on the
entire stripe. And connect the DATA from the disconnected segment to
GPIO 17. You can of course also cut the stripe and connect the power
lines to the power supply with the recommended cross-section. (AWG 20 or
22) Both the +5 volts and the ground lines/GND, which are available
separately on the stripe, can be connected to the power supply unit
using separate WAGO connection terminals. Your LG does not require any
extra drivers for the RP2040, see webOS Debug. (edited)

Hi, You have to activate the multi-segment in CMakeList.txt and
recompile. See: https://github.com/awawa-dev/HyperSerialPico “Pinout can
be changed, but you need to make changes to CMakeList.txt (e.g.
OUTPUT_DATA_PIN / OUTPUT_SPI_DATA_PIN / OUTPUT_SPI_CLOCK_PIN) and
recompile the project.
Also, multi-segment mode can be enabled in this file: SECOND_SEGMENT_INDEX option at the beginning and optionally SECOND_SEGMENT_REVERSED. Once
compiled, the results can be found in the firmware folder.”
You probably also need to set up a second instance in HyperHDR with the missing segment.

mr.meorzipan 05/05/2024 8:01 PM
Buy a esp32 or rp2040 (rp2040 better) with USB micro, or some clone with
USB C with a nicer color than icky green. They are all fairly cheap too.
Then no need to solder a USB wire. Alternatively you could solder a
female USB C port. Or you mean some premade thing with some other
controller with led strip with a specific software and stuff? If you
still have yet to buy everything, i suggest you do it right and buy a
SK6812 CW(cold white...no it's not really cold, it's near 6500K), a
RP2040 with built-in 3.3v > 5v level shifter(i suggest buying the
Adafruit Feather Scorpio, it allows 8 data pins through the level
shifter, so you can do multi segment setups), a 5v power supply that can
feed the SK6812 the current it needs (calculated at 100% brightness and
the amount of leds present), a fuse to protect the led strip, a 1000ohm
capacitor, and a aluminium or wooden frame to mount or stick the led
strip instead of sticking it on your TV. (edited)
