there's a fun design flaw in mtk pkgs all data in a pak aligned to 16
bytes is AES encrypted, but for some reason, they don't use any standard
padding, and the data that's left unaligned is just XORed with the AES
KEY! so, for example if most of last 16 bytes were left unaligned, and
you know the plaintext(or use your brain) you can recover most of the
key, and you'll just have to brute force the rest. (if you can recover
12 bytes, you can just brute force 4 which is trivial). IV can be easily
recovered too by the "reserved mtk inc" header. this way we can have
some cool new mtk pkg keys:
("DB7D510A8DA635645A844B2F55C0B5BA", "93A97FF5C4BCE585122C7109FC2E1A3F", "Sony 2017 Linux"), ("9320FC32BCDCE3178B7B2BA2A1AF3391", "D93F84B9BDDC9E1BD3E7C114AAA2FAA3", "Sony 2015 Android"), ("F888FC0268C6692D7EB76243E53D47E5", "EE40FE4700EC3197336940D4B30D4BE8", "Sony 2016 Android"), ("07A30C2E4AAE4849EC3DD2301C381868", "BC1443A0D17AAB2DB1EA0302EF280717", "Philips Android")
The philips key seems to work from 2017 up to 2022 (worked on
TPM171E(2017), TPM191E(2019), TPM211E(2021)) although to add this to
epk2extract it would need modification to work with 0x170 header, right
now it will fail when you add these keys

it quickly turns out there is a follow up problem to deal with. It often
(like in every recording) happens that my device (LG WebOS25) produces
PIF file that references the same STR file multiple times, so PIF
handler includes it to output TS multiple times as well which makes the
output unusable. To circumvent that I have implemented a custom fix to
make PIF handler skip already processed STR files. With that, PIF/STR
extraction seems to work well in my conditions.
