01/21/2024 2:21 AM
those (ground, power, SCL, SDA) are the only four connections you need
to make. the only other one that could be useful is write protect (WP),
but i've always seen that permanently grounded (meaning writing is
enabled) on LG boards.
01/21/2024 2:19 AM
the only other signals you need to connect are the I2C clock (SCL) and
I2C data (SDA). SDA is pin 5 and SCL is pin 6. while you could
theoretically connect somewhere else on the same I2C bus, it would only
be worth trying if the EEPROM were inaccessible and you had schematics.
01/21/2024 2:15 AM
pin 8 is power, which goes under various names like Vcc/Vdd or in this
case (as this is a 3.3 V system) something like +3.3V/3V3. you could
technically also just connect to anywhere else on the same 3.3 V power
rail (in the B6P schematic it's called +3.3V_NORMAL). i believe signal
integrity should be less of an issue, but there are other problems. so
unless you have a multimeter to figure out where else it's accessible
and/or a good reason to use somewhere else, stay with pin 8. (edited)
hellobox. 01/21/2024 2:07 AM
pin 4 is ground (GND). most of the time, pins 1-3 will be either
connected to ground or left unconnected. all the LG boards i've seen
have had pins 1-4 of their NVM EEPROM grounded. so you could connect
your programmer's ground to any of them, or even any other ground on the
board like the heatsink or mounting holes. but closer is better for
signal integrity, so use pin 4 if possible. (edited)
01/21/2024 2:04 AM

you can see the pinout in the

white dot on the board both indicate pin 1. also, pin 1 is usually in

along one side, then straight across and back along the other side in
