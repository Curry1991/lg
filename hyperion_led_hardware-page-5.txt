satdx62 01/26/2025 9:23 PM
Hi, none of my DIY solutions have failed so far. Neither as an external
solution with USB Grabber on the Raspberry Pi, nor as an internal
solution on my two LG devices. The Ambilight on my oldest LG, built in
2017, runs for at least 12 hours almost every day and still works
perfectly. There is virtually no off-the-shelf solution that comes with
all components plug-in play. And if you find one like the ‘Insanelight
Wemos D1 Mini WLED RGBW’, which you can configure yourself, see
https://insanelight.de/shop/insanelight-wled/, the hardware does not
fulfil all the requirements for a well-coordinated Ambilight. Much more
expensive HDFury Diva incl. ambient light:
https://insanelight.de/shop/diva/ Even expensive systems such as the
HDFury Diva for well over €400 or the Philips Hue Play HDMI Sync Box for
around €260 are supplied without LEDs, power supply unit and all
accessories, let alone suitable software. So either do it yourself or
switch to a Philips TV with integrated Ambilight. Incidentally, I bought
one of these Philips Ambilight smart TVs as a trial and immediately
realised that the advertised Ambilight looked very imprecise and awful
compared to my own DIY Ambilight. So I gave it to my son as a present.

mr.meorzipan 01/19/2025 5:19 AM
Depends on your setup, how many leds will be used, you may or may not
benefit from multi-segment wiring. You can use a ordinary RP2040,
however, you'd need a fast level shifter like a SN74AHCT125N to turn
that 3.3v data output into 5v, cause the SK6812 needs 5v for data line,
otherwise you get instability/flickering. Or you take the easier road
and get a RP2040 with a built-in 3.3v>5v level shifter. For a list of
supported RP2040's with built-in level shifter, check this:
https://github.com/awawa-dev/HyperHDR/discussions/561 To get an idea
what i mean by multi-segment, it means to connect led strip in segments.
This means to half the led strip and connect each half seperately to
it's own data lane on the RP2040, to decrease the latency, so you get a
higher refresh rate for the leds, cause the more leds on a single long
strip, the higher the latency. SK6812 has aside RGB, also white led,
which adds latency. So like, with a 55" TV: - 30 leds per meter or 116
leds latency 4.6ms - 60 leds per meter or 232 leds latency 9,3ms For
77": - 30 leds per meter or 161 leds latency 6,4ms - 60 leds per meter
or 322 leds latency 13ms (You'll likely use less leds, i just took the
exact height and width of the TV, for the leds) At 10ms, it'd be 100hz,
at 8.3ms 120hz, at 13ms it's 77hz. So if you like to game near 120hz,
and the amount of leds you have exceed that, if chosing a RP2040 with
level shifter, it may make sense to use a model with more than 1 data
lane output. Otherwise, any rp2040 with built-in 5v>3.3v level shifter
in that list would probably be fine. If you're planning to get a bigger
TV in the future, and use a ledstrip with 60 leds per meter, then you
could consider one with more than 1 data lane output on the level
shifter. Also, in case you haven't ordered that SK6812 yet, just
planning to, get the CW (Cold White) version instead. It's actually more
neutral/closer to D65 than NW/(Neutral White), which is actually warm.
(edited)

As for the automotive fuse, it is intended, in case a failure occurs,
because of your mistake or whatever, so if somewhere on the line to and
from the leds, something suddenly pulls more than 12A or 15A (whatever
the fuse is rated to), maybe a bad short circuit, then the fuse kills
itself and power is cut before really bad damage happens to your leds
and PSU. (They may still be damaged, but at least there's less chance
for melting and fire and explosion). (edited)
01/17/2025 1:35 PM
As for the wire, according to ohm's law, where you have voltage, current
and resistance affecting eachother, you'll need at least a cross
diameter of 1mm² of copper, otherwise the wire will heat up and the PSU
has to push more current, as it does not fit well over the wire,
stressing the PSU and causing a voltage drop for the leds. (edited)
01/17/2025 1:32 PM
Anyway, as you can see, with 240 leds, the PSU would need to be capable
of pushing 12A if the leds are set to 100%. In regular use, you will be
using the leds at 50% or less probably, but you must always prepare for
100%. I would recommend using slightly larger PSU than 12A though, in
that situation. (edited)

it easy to connect/disconnect all wires (you can also opt for crimping
connections if you wish, but Wago's are quite reliable). Electricial
knowledge (including ohm's law) and soldering experience are
recommended. This will let you have your syncing leds, that will work
Anywhere in the TV, apps, TV channels, etc, not just for a single HDMI
device. However, DRM protected content can not be captured by piccap, so
you will have to use an external HDMI device for that, a like a
Chromecast with Google TV, or Nvidia Shield Pro. Upside with external
device, is Shield Pro can do pretty much any codec, like Dolby TrueHD
and DTS Master, and supports a lot of Dolby Vision profiles. However, it
does not support hw decode of VP9 profile 2, nor AV1, so no HDR on
YouTube (after all, the soc of Shield Pro is actually quite old), but
chances are likely your LG supports VP9 profile 2 and will get HDR on
YouTube, so it's mostly fine if you think about it. The syncing leds
will work well, even if you use on your TV things like 3840x2160
resolution, 120hz refreshrate, VRR, HDR10 or Dolby Vision, and color can
also get close to what's on-screen. (edited)

satdx62 01/13/2025 10:58 PM
No additional kernel driver is required for HyperSerialPico on the
Adafruit Raspberry Pi Pico RP2040. It is automatically recognised by the
operating system. HyperSerialPico is the time of the best solution in
combination with HyperHDR and SK6812RGBW LEDs. PicCap can also deliver
the data to HyperSPI on Raspberry Pi, but this is more for people using
a combination of Raspberry Pi and external USB grabbers. In addition,
the communication does not take place on the same local host and
therefore there may be delays and therefore no speed advantage like
PicCap HyperHDR on the same host. Multisegment brings speed advantages
if you want to control more than 300 LEDs. This is automatically the
case when using LED strips with 144 LEDs per metre. 60 LEDs per metre
are more than enough for a distance to the wall of up to 40 cm. The best
results in terms of colour fidelity are achieved by the SK6812 RGBW in
the CW version. (Cold White) See:
https://github.com/satgit62/How-to-Install-and-set-up-Ambilight-on-LG-webOS?tab=readme-ov-file#logs

available in black or white. That's up to him, it's about aesthetics and
