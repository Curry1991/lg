satdx62 06/27/2026 9:53 AM
You must install a level shifter for both standard RP2040 Pico and ESP32
boards. Level shifters are required because RP2040 Pico and ESP32 boards
output data at a logic voltage of 3.3 V, while SK6812RGBW and WS2812B
LEDs require 5 V to recognize a “HIGH” signal. If you do not use a level
shifter, you risk various unpredictable behaviors from your LED strip:
Flickering or incorrect colors: The LEDs mistakenly interpret the 3.3 V
signal as noise, causing erroneous data to propagate along the strip.
Failed LEDs: LEDs further down the strip may not light up at all due to
voltage drop and signal degradation. And yes, you can also use boards
with a USB-C connection, just like with WS2812B RGB LEDs. However, the
WS2812B LEDs do not offer the same color quality as the RGBW LEDs. The
RP2040s I recommend already have a level shifter built in, so you don’t
need to install an external one.

satdx62 05/24/2026 9:32 AM
ItsyBitsy, Plasma 2040, and RP2040 Feather Scorpio are all based on the
same RP2040 hardware and are currently the best HyperSerial/Pico devices
available. Furthermore, they do not require special kernel drivers, as
is the case with ESPs. There are no dropouts at serial transfer rates of
up to 5 MB. Another advantage is that the firmware has been specifically
optimized for HyperHDR and delivers the best results. The ItsyBitsy 2040
has only one data output and is therefore suitable for setups with up to
300 LEDs, while the Plasma 2040, with two data outputs, and the RP2040
Feather Scorpio, with 8 data outputs, are MultiSegment-compatible and
can therefore easily control a higher number of LEDs (900 RGBW LEDs).
See:https://github.com/awawa-dev/HyperSerialPico#some-benchmark-results

Hello PicCap, Hyperion.NG, and HyperHDR users. I have a suggestion that
would help keep both users and developers informed about the settings
required for PicCap (hyperion-webos) to work on different versions of
webOS. A long time ago, @Informatic started a thread (Version Support
Tracker #16) at https://github.com/webosbrew/hyperion-webos/issues/16
where users reported which settings got the webOS recording feature
working. At the time, this was a very informative resource that
contributed to further development, among other things. Unfortunately,
this was not pursued further, and the user reports ended with webOS TV
version 6.2.0-39 and software version 03.21.40. I would be very grateful
if you could help revive this treasure trove of information so that,
first, we can contribute to further development with the collected data,
and second, we can help new users. You can retrieve the important system
information using the following command in SSH/Terminal:
    grep -h -E '(hardware_id|core_os_release|product_id|webos_manufacturing_version|board_type)' /var/run/nyx/*
At https://github.com/webosbrew/hyperion-webos/issues/16, you’ll find
the appropriate template along with guidelines on what information it
should include. Since more options are currently available, you should
also specify whether you use Quirks and, if so, which ones. Since AI is
now also used in newer devices, you should also specify whether and with
which AI features data collection works or does not work. All of this
information would be very helpful and represents a win-win situation for
developers and users. Thank you in advance to all users who would like
to join this initiative. (edited)

Hi, since the LEDs are typically powered externally and not via the
controller/ESP, RP2040, or RP3550 board, it doesn't matter whether you
use 12-V or 24-V LEDs. The controller board is powered via USB, and only
the GND and DATA-Out pins are connected to the LEDs and the positive and
negative terminals of the power supply. Even 12V and 24V LEDs require
the same data level as 5V LEDs. 12-V SK6812RGBW LED strips (CW, NW) are
also available, where each individual LED is controllable. But as I
said, in practice, the 5-volt LEDs never reach the maximum power
consumption values predicted by the LED calculator. In addition, I hid
my power supply behind the sideboard and ran 2x1-mm electrical cable to
the WAGO terminals behind the TV. The red and white connection cables
from the LED inputs and outputs are plugged into the WAGO terminals.
Since they are extremely short, there is no cause for concern. They are
supplied this way by the manufacturer and pose no danger.

Hello, Yes, it is well known that Dolby Vision can cause issues and
incorrect color reproduction due to the various profiles. This depends
on the source and the Dolby Vision content being played. To achieve 100%
accurate color reproduction, a new DV-LUT must be created. To do this,
the source used to play Dolby Vision videos must be recalibrated on the
respective LG SoC. If that is not possible, there are two options. There
are two options: 1. Use a universal LUT for DV that can handle all DV
profiles. (The success rate is approximately 75–90%) 2. Create a
separate color profile using a JSON file. This is sent as a POST request
to the IP:PORT/json-rpc endpoint of the RPC HyperHDR service and used as
an HA integration, or remapped to an unused remote control button as a
CURL command using the input hook. If you would like to test a universal
Dolby Vision LUT, please contact me via private message. (edited)
