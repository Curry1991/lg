SORRY! Best of the YouTube HowTO at 2:59 explaining that 3V is 5V. The
PIN is measured at 3.3V. The PIN does not belong on the LED and neither
does the G (GND). You can do this to test a few LEDs, since the nodeMCU
(ESP8266) is supplied via USB, there are only 5V 500 to 900mA at the
nodeMCU, depending on whether it was connected to USB 2.0 or 3.0. VIN
and G (GND) can be connected to the power supply of the stripes G (GND)
must have a common GND with all (nodeMCU, power supply, stripes). D4 is
then DATA and goes to DIN (Data IN > arrow direction) of the stripes.
Possibly D3 if you have stripes that also need clock (CI Clock IN >
arrow direction). In the WLED configuration, you must not only enter the
number of LEDs, but also the stripe type, the GPIO and color order used
should be checked/set. And so there are more 'inaccuracies' in this
video. (edited)

awawa_dev 07/01/2022 7:37 PM
You have sources available. It's called "linear smoothing" based on
hyperion and alternative tuned by myself which should be faster for same
parameters as testing proved (reduced decay effect at the end of target
processing). Practically both in fact have little common with "linear"
term in practice and the higher source refresh rate/the more capable LED
device (true high refresh rate capable) the better/faster behavior. Also
when use with piccap on TV directly you must understand that the
captured image is already behind the TV and smoothing may cause the lag
even worse. When use with USB grabbers even including grabber processing
(usually around 50-70ms + few ms from HyperHDR) we are ahead of the
typical TV (excluding gaming mode etc) and there is more room for
smoothing. So probably you must reduce delay for smoothing or turn it
off when use HyperHDR directly on the TV.
