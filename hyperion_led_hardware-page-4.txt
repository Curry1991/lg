satdx62 05/01/2025 6:58 PM
I hope they have also thought of a level shifter like SN74AHCT125N and
small parts. If not, you can try the trick with the sacrificial LED and
diode first and see if everything works. (Although I always prefer a
level shifter) With a little trick (hack), a 3.3 V microcontroller
without a level shifter can be used to control 5 V LEDs with a single
diode and a sacrificial LED. The first LED is lowered to 4.3 V by the
diode so that a signal of 4.3 V * 0.7 = 3.01 V can be used for control.
The logic of this LED is 4.3 V, which is sufficient to supply the other
LEDs with 5 V. Pay attention to the actual pin arrangement of your LED
strips such as + 5V, GND and Data in (DI). The arrangement may vary
depending on the LED strips used.

archives for your TV model, and look for the kernel source and the
toolchain. Install the toolchain somewhere. Run/source its environment
script. Extract the kernel source. Get /proc/config.gz from the TV,
decompress it, rename it to .config in the root of the kernel source
tree. (Not 100% sure about the rest; going from memory and it may vary.)

you want. Then you should be able to build the module with like
make path/to/module.ko or something. You can potentially use
buildroot-nc4 instead of LG's toolchain, but I wouldn't recommend it.

tools. I believe there is a oneliner to do it somewhere around here.

satdx62 03/31/2025 8:11 PM
The distance between the LEDs and the wall also depends on the number of
LEDs per meter. For direct wall mounting with a distance of 10 to 20 cm
from the wall, 60 LEDs per meter should be used. If the device is
mounted on its own stand and the distance to the wall is greater than 25
cm, 30 LEDs per meter can also be used, as the illuminated area is
larger at a greater distance to the wall than at a shorter distance. The
geometry of the LEDs mounted on the device or on frames can be set
precisely under “Layout Advanced Settings”. Whether directly on the
outer edge of the device or offset by a few centimeters, it doesn't
matter, you can make precise adjustments. Even trapezoidal or zigzag
corrections are possible. In Layout, you can even take into account
whether LEDs are missing or not at the corners due to the wiring or
whether the LED strips overlap. Unfortunately, not every user is aware
of these settings and wonders why something is wrong with the geometry
of the LEDs. See section LED layout:

satdx62 03/16/2025 1:44 PM
HyperSerial, like HyperSerialPico, is an alternative LED controller
firmware that enables a higher transfer rate and takes place directly
via the USB interface of the TV and not via the Wi-Fi or LAN. This means
fewer errors and delays. As the ESPs have different serial chips, you
have to make sure that the TV has the right driver to control it. The
RP2040 is also a standalone LED controller like the ESP that works with
HyperSerialPico firmware and not with WLED.
