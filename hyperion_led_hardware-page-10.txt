most ws2812b works without level shifter, if u check the strip specs,
there are "design margins": V-IH (min) and V-IL (max) input voltage,
e.g. sk6812 VIH is about 3.4V (so it requires 3.4V for 'high' signal,
but your mcu logic voltage level is only 3.3V, so probably it wont work)
but ws2812b have lower VIH and VIL , so if u have short data cable
probably it works (mostly reliably :D) without shifter btw avoid ip
rated strips..  also ws2812b not the best choice for ambilight (IMHO)

To attach the led strip to your TV, you can just stick it behind the TV
if you want, but maybe best for heat dissipation to mount on a aluminium
frame, so the leds may live longer since heat is enemy of LED, and also
so heat doesn't go in OLED pixels (if you use OLED display), which do
not like heat either. You can choose to use some aluminium frame that is
30 degree angled, so light spreads more outward, instead of a lot of the
light going behind the TV and possible diffusing where you don't want
it, but it may depend on how far from the wall and the brightness set
(chances are you can set a lower brightness) if 30° angle will work
well. (edited)
[mr.meorzipan]
mr.meorzipan 08/02/2023 4:19 AM
For the led strip, from the type of LED strip and it's length, eg
depending on how much power it uses, you want choose your power supply.
5v SK6812 RGB CW LED strips are what people here usually choose now, it
has RGB and a cold white led(its not actually cold white, it's neutral,
the other warmer led strip options are actually more orange than
neutral). Use the LED strip without any special extra water protection
like sillicon, so just get the IP30 ones, cause the silicon would make
it harder for the leds to dissipate heat and it'll diffuse light, that's
a nono. For the power supply, lets say you got yourself a 5 Volt SK6812
RGBW 5 meter LED strip with 60 leds, which would be 300 leds, on 100%
usage it'd use about 76 Watt. You likely won't use the led strip at
100%, wayyy to bright, maybe more like 50% or so, so it'd use way less
power...however, just to give yourself some freedom, and so you won't
accidentally fry your power supply if you set the led at 100% the other
day when you're drunk or something, i'd get a power supply that can
handle that 100%, and maybe a little beyond. Btw, you actually do not
want to drive your LEDs at 100%, will greatly decrease lifespan over
time, you want to actually underdrive LEDs, at 50% or ideally even less
if possible. So for peace of mind i'd just get a 5v 20A (100 Watt) power
supply. In case you got yourself a 5 Volt SK6812 RGBW 5 meter strip with
30 leds, about 150 leds, you could go for a 5v 10A or 15A power supply
(just get 15A). Depending how close to wall the TV is, 30 leds or 60
leds per meter look better, be easier pick out details, or be brighter.
30 leds may be little easier to pick out detail, 60 leds looks like
details are more fused into each other, further away from wall you may
want 60 leds for the extra brightness. 30 leds per meter is cheaper to
buy and also cheaper in power usage than 60 leds per meter, the refresh
rate is also slightly higher with less leds, especially if you install
as multi segmented. (edited)

Seems that guide has no inclusion about the Raspberri RP2040, for
HyperSerialPico, which Awawa is recommending for being able to produce a
high quality and precise signal, being great for SK6812.
https://github.com/awawa-dev/HyperSerialPico Perhaps it's an slightly
older version of this guide?
https://www.hyperhdr.eu/2023/02/ultimate-guide-on-how-to-build-led.html?m=1#chapter2g
Anyway, it depends on how you want to do this and what TV you use. If
like you say, you have a LG TV with WebOS, if it's fast enough, after
you attained root privileges, you can run install Piccap and HyperHDR
from homebrew channel. Be sure to never update to a software/firmware
version that removes or severely restricts your ability to root your
TV... Piccap which will grab/record the display and start a server which
something can listen to. HyperHDR can connect and listen to Piccap's
server, either running local in the TV or remotely on a Raspberri Pi,
digest the video from Piccap and convert it to something the led driver
can use Then HyperHDR can send the output via the USB port to a ESP32 or
Raspberri RP2040, which will drive your led strip. Since the led strip
output's data with 3.3v, but led strips use 5v or more for data, you
want a fast 3.3v>5v level shifter to avoid problems, for example, the
SN74AHCT125. Some Raspberri RP2040's have a 3.3v>5v level shifter
built-in, Awawa has some listed on his HyperSerialPico Github page.
