mr.meorzipan 11/24/2023 8:01 PM
Watt = voltage x current(also called 'amperage') The power supply
transforms incoming power to a different voltage and current. Current is
electricity that gets pulled over the cable, voltage is the pressure (or
the potential difference between 2 points). Pulling means that only the
current that is actually needed by whatever needs it, will be pulled. If
a PSU outputs 20Amp at 5v, that means it outputs 100 Watt, to get there,
it can transform the 220v from your mains power and pull 0,455Amp, cause
220x0,455 is 100. However, transforming power may not be 100% efficient,
so there can be a certain percentage of power loss, meaning the power
supply needs to pull more current to make up for the loss, hence
@hellobox's example of bad case of 33% efficiency, which would mean a
power supply would need to additionally pull more than half the current
to get to 100 watt of power being output. So you'd get close to double
power usage in your electricity bill to be able to power your ledstrip.
Also, for every loss of power, whether it be in the power supply, or the
cable, that power is lost as heat, meaning if a cable where to have a
too high resistance(eg, is harder for electricity to move over a
conductive material) due to being too thin, long or due to the material,
for current to travel over, or a component like a resistor couldn't
dissipate the power, it may heat up a little, or a lot, or burn or melt
something. Also, if resistance is too high due to a cable being too thin
and not conductive enough(or something between lowers conductivity) for
the amount of current that you plan to travel over it, not only may a
cable heat up a lot, but a power supply may need to send more power to
compensate for power loss over the cable, so the device that pulls power
can get it's requested current and voltage, so if the power supply can't
handle the amount of current send, it may overheat. Additionally you may
get a voltage drop, which may cause the requesting device to misbehave
(edited)
