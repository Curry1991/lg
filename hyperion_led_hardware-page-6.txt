satdx62 11/13/2024 7:43 PM
Hello, If you can root your new LG, there is nothing wrong with running
PicCap and HyperHDR/Hyperion together on the LG. If the PicCap/HyperHDR
is set correctly, the functionality of your LG will not be affected in
any way. Please note, however, that PicCap cannot process DRM-protected
video content from internal apps on new devices, so no Ambilight either.
This DRM restriction does not apply to video content played via HDMI
such as FireTV, Apple TV, Playstation and so on. Hosting the
HyperHDR/Hyperion on a Raspberry Pi Zero 2W is possible, but it doesn't
bring any real advantage over your TV since the hardware is very
limited. A Raspberry Pi 4 is an advantage here. The LEDs can be
controlled directly by the Pi in SPI mode, but there is a delay in the
data transfer from PicCap to Hyperion. LEDs controller based on
HyperSerial/HyperSerialPico, operated directly on the LG Device via USB,
thanks to the ultra-fast transmission protocol, is the better choice in
conjunction with HyperHDR and the delays are reduced to a minimum and
can hardly be measured.

satdx62 11/11/2024 1:27 PM
The procedure for using HyperHDR servers is identical. The HDMI signal
is connected to the HDMI-USB grabber using an HDMI splitter, the USB
output of the grabber is connected to the device where HyperHDR is
hosted. In this case, the Synology NAS. HyperHDR forwards the data to
the LED controller. Ideally, you should use an LED controller that
communicates with HyperHDR via Wi-Fi, like all ESP-based controllers. To
avoid transmission errors via USB from Grabber, the NAS should be
located near your TV. In my opinion, using a Raspberry Pi is the best
HyperHDR server solution. It offers the necessary computing power and
many connections so that every conceivable setup variant can be
realized. (edited)

satdx62 10/23/2024 8:34 PM
As not all x LEDs are controlled simultaneously at full brightness with
a dynamic Ambilight, the two feeds are sufficient for your 65-inch
device. In rare cases, 14 A will be required. In my case, it is even
less than 10 A. With a static Ambilight, e.g. via WLED, it can happen
that with full white brightness and the suggested current output of
14-20A and with the correct cross-section of the cables used, a third
feed is required at the same time. The use of the correct fuse should
therefore not be neglected. The WLED calculator should be used for this.
See: https://wled-calculator.github.io/ It contains all the relevant
data based on the number of the LEDs used and the type.

Hi, Yes, the power supply for the LEDs +5 volts and -5 volts (GND) must
be applied both at the start of the LED strip and at the end of the LED
strip. For my 65-inch device, the two feeds are sufficient. For a
75-inch device, a third feed would have to be made in the middle of the
LED strip. And of course, the DATA output (GPIO 16) and GND of the
Adafruit RP2040 Scorpio LED controller must be connected to the data and
GND input of the LEDs in order to be able to control the LEDs. As can be
seen in the picture. The Adafruit LED controller is supplied with power
(+5 volts) by LG via USB. You must connect everything as shown in the
picture, provided you have transferred the correct firmware to the

further action in WLED. All other screenshots from them were OK. Which
“Fps” values do you mean and from which logs? The fps rate that arrives
at the LEDs depends on several factors. One is the maximum fps set in
PicCap, and the other is the refresh time you have set in HyperHDR under
smoothing. If the refresh rate is set higher than the LED strip driver
capabilities, this can lead to delays and processing bottlenecks. So a
higher update time is not always appropriate. This can also lead to a
higher CPU load on the TVs. An average of approx. 60 fps is a good
value.

[] alleinka Greetings, in case I will use the quinled diguno v3 I do not
need a level shifter, correct: Level S...
satdx62 09/04/2024 12:55 PM
When using the “quinled diguno v3” controller, you do not need a level
shifter, as this is already installed. In addition to the 3-pin
plug/coupling, the LED strips have a red wire for + 5Volt and a white
wire for GND at the beginning and end, so that two feeds/power supply
are easily possible at the beginning and end. If you require a third
power supply, you must solder the connection cable to the marked 5Volt
and GND points in the middle of the LED strip. In practice, this would
mean that you would have to lay a cable from the power supply unit to
behind the TV, and from there distribute the current to the respective
feed point using WAGO terminals.

Greetings, in case I will use the quinled diguno v3 I do not need a
level shifter, correct: Level Shifter For those who do not use a
ready-made LED controller with a built-in level shifter, I strongly
recommend integrating a level shifter into the circuit. Since most LEDs
need to be supplied with 5 volts, but the logic of the controller can
only handle 3.2 volts, the direct DATA line should be connected via a
level shifter. For testing purposes, you can temporarily install a 470
Ohm resistor on the DATA line and a 1000µF electrolytic capacitor on the
5 Volt+ and GND at the input of the LEDs. (edited)

satdx62 08/10/2024 9:14 AM
Yes, the Mag WLED-1 controller performs better than the ESP 8266 because
it is based on a powerful ESP32. However, the LEDs must be supplied with
power separately, as the board of the Mag WLED-1 allows a maximum power
consumption of 3 amps at 5 volts. The
Quinled-dig-uno-v3-digital-LED-controller is itself supplied via a
powerful power supply unit and the power consumption here is up to 14
amps, so that the LEDs can be connected directly to the controller. It
can also be purchased with LAN or upgraded if the Wi-Fi connection does
not work freely. If you can/want to do without WLED, then I recommend an
RP2040 with a built-in level shifter such as Adafruit ItsyBitsy RP2040,
PLASMA 2040 or RP2040 Adalight Feather Scorpio. The advantage of these
controllers are the fast CPU and serial transfer, via USB, with the
HyperSerialPico protocol. There is no lag. The new ESP32 S2 Mini also
complies with the awawa-dev specifications and can be flashed with
HyperSerial firmware. I have also tested it without a level shifter, and
it works very well. However, I recommend installing a level shifter
between GPIO and LED-DATA to avoid flickering. See the connection
examples
at:https://github.com/satgit62/How-to-Install-and-set-up-Ambilight-on-LG-webOS#example-connection-of-various-led-controllers

[] alleinka Hello. What is the difference between Hyperion and HyperHdr?

satdx62 08/07/2024 10:04 PM
It depends on which hardware you want to use. HyperHDR is a fork of
Hyperion. To realize Ambilight on LG TVs, you need either Hyperion.NG or
HyperHDR in addition to PicCap. Both Hyperion versions are similar in
structure and operation, and each has its own special functions. And as
always, there are advantages and disadvantages. It also depends on which
LED controller you want to use. If you want to use LED controllers such
as HyperSerial, HyperSerialPico on RP2040 or HyperSerial with WLED on
ESP32 and attach importance to HDR content, you should use HyperHDR. For
LED controllers with pure WLED firmware on ESP32, you can use
Hyperion.NG. Hardware support for LED lighting is also important. Both
solutions support most common RGB/W LEDs. However, since there are also
exotic RGB lighting, you can find out more in advance on the GitHub
page. HyperHDR: https://github.com/awawa-dev/HyperHDR Hyperion.NG:
https://github.com/hyperion-project/hyperion.ng (edited)

DATA and CLK. The firmware (HyperSerialPico) must be compiled correctly

15) for data line if single LED segment is used. Output is DA connector.
Compile with set(OUTPUT_DATA_PIN 14) for data line if two LED segments
are used. Output is: CL (first segment) and DA (second segment)
connector. For SPI LED strips, spi1 interface must be used:

set(SPI_INTERFACE spi1) Output is: CL (clock) and DA (data) connector.
