satdx62 05/03/2025 8:50 AM
The WAGO connection block is intended exclusively for power distribution
at the beginning and end of the LEDs. The additional red +5V and white
GND wires without connectors at the beginning and end of the LED strips
are for the power supply. See my drawing:
https://github.com/satgit62/How-to-Install-and-set-up-Ambilight-on-LG-webOS?tab=readme-ov-file#rp2040-adalight-feather-scorpio-mit-level-shifter-ausgang-gpio-16
The 3-pin adapter (red, white and green) supplied with the LEDs is only
intended for direct connection to the HyperSerialPico. (Make sure that
the green line DATA input of the LEDs has direct contact with the GPIO
output of the RP2040 and white to GND, the red wire remains free). The
HyperHDR protocol looks OK so far. As far as the arrangement of the LEDs
is concerned, I would arrange them exactly, e.g. the first LED
approximately where the actual start is. However, since you have placed
your first LED in the bottom area, you have to calculate the arrangement
differently (Input Position/This is your first led). In this case,
perhaps, 118 and not 114. (edited)

out the PID when executing the top command. Press Ctrl + C to end the

satdx62 04/27/2025 11:43 PM
You do not need to activate all of PicCap's Quirks. Only those that are
required for your device. You can also test without. In PicCap, 60 fps
is sufficient. A resolution that is set too high can also cause delays.
In HyperHDR, set 60 fps under Smoothing for the time being and use the
alternative Linear (faster) under Type. If you set the refresh rate too
high for the LED strip driver, delays and processing bottlenecks may
occur. The transmission away to the LED controller also plays an
important role. You have not mentioned which LED controllers operate.
Wi-Fi or serial via USB? (edited)
[satdx62]
[] bahoue Wled
satdx62 04/27/2025 11:22 PM
For WLED, any ESP32 NodeMCU Module WLAN Wi-Fi Dev Kit C development
board with the CP2102 serial chip can be used, as the transmission takes
place via Wi-Fi. If you have a LAN connection behind the TV, you can use
an ESP with a built-in LAN module, such as the ESP32 WT32-ETH01. CP2102
is too slow for HyperSerial, so a CH340 or CH9102 must be used, as the
transfer takes place via USB. However, additional kernel drivers must be
installed for CH340 and CH9102. A good alternative to the ESP32 with
CH340 and CH9102 is the ESP32 S2 Mini, which does not require any
additional kernel drivers from LG. Be aware that in addition to the ESP
itself, you will need additional components for smooth operation,
including a level shifter. My favorite is definitely HyperSerialPico on
RP2040 with built-in level shifter. (edited)

flash memory. The compressed LUTs require less than 25 MB and the
quality is the same as the uncompressed LUTs.

satdx62 04/03/2025 5:17 PM
I have nothing against the WS2815, except for the disadvantage that,
like the WS2812b, it only has 3 channels (RGB), unlike the SK6812
(RGB+W), which has an additional white channel and is now also available
as a 12 volt version. The additional white channel ensures better color
rendition. In addition, the SK6812RGBW 5/12 Volt are available in both
NW (natural white) and CW (cold white) versions and depending on the LED
controller used can be better adjusted to achieve the most natural color
rendering. Speaks blue cast or red cast. At 7 W, the standby power
consumption of the WS2815 (12 V) is higher than the standby power
consumption of the SK6812RGBW (5 V) at 1,440 W when using 300 LEDs, for
example. The only advantage of the WS2815 is that it has an additional
backup data line and is therefore more fail-safe if it is mounted in an
inaccessible place. PS: I am pleased that you are happy with the
result. (edited)
