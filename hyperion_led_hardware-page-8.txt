[] _phyte 1 more gerneral question: How can I classify the three
programmes HyperHDR-Hyperion-Piccap? and how ...
mr.meorzipan 04/26/2024 4:18 AM
Piccap captures the display, runs a server which can run as a local
stream or on a port for network access. Don't worry about the resolution
being very low, you set it for the amount of leds. HyperHDR or
Hyperion.ng take the stream of piccap, and processes it such that when
it is send to the led controller, the leds get the right information. If
you let it all happen on the TV itself, and going out the USB port, you
get the lowest latency and easiest setup that works even of the TV gets
no internet connection, you won't need stupid and very expensive hdmi
2.0b or 2.1 splitters with bunch of issues and possibly no HDCP support,
it'll work with HDR10, Dolby Vision, any framerate, etc. If you do multi
segment with 2 halves, one going left, one going right, instead of one
long ass led strip, you cut refresh time in half. And could maybe even
do longer lengths than 5 meter (for larger TV's) before getting voltage
drop issues due to very long lenghts, maybe if needed, by 4 halves? Just
make sure if using a rp2040 with 3.3v to 5v level shifter, exposes more
than 1 data pin. The rp2040 by itself has plenty gpio data output's, but
the built-in level shifter of some rp2040's may expose only 1, instead
of 2 or 8. Luckily your Adafruit Feather RP2040 Scorpi exposes 8 data
pins through the level shifter. (edited)

mr.meorzipan 04/05/2024 12:59 AM
If they are too thin and made of aluminium instead of copper, they may
have too much resistance, so if too much current(amperage) goes over
them then can handle, power is lost in the wire, which will heat the
wire, snd the PSU will have to handle more current to make up for the
loss. The leds might also get a lower voltage due to power loss. The
result in bad cases may be that the insulation or the wire itself could
burn, the PSU could die of handling too much current (or the fuse kills
itself) and the leds would not be able to function correctly or reduce
lifespan. So use appropriate thickness and material of wire to get at
least the minimum or higher amount of conductiveness you need. Even
something like copper clad aluminium can technically work btw, you just
want double the thickness that you would otherwise use with copper wire,
since alumiunium is just less conductive. (edited)

through them. (edited)

on the LG hosted HyperHDR. For example, you had to start the whole
procedure using a Windows computer that is capable of displaying HDR and

satdx62 03/24/2024 4:13 PM
Hi, You can disregard the LUT table if you use PicCap and HyperHDR or
Hyperion on the TV. The image content is provided by “Hyperion webOS”
(internal grabber), PicCap speaks. The captured image area is passed to
Hyperion's “flatbuffer” and is applied to a LUT “flat_lut_lin_tables.3d”
specially created for LG, which is automatically obtained when HyperHDR
is installed. In case of using an external grabber HDMI to USB on a
Raspberry Pi, you also need to use the appropriate LUT table for the
grabber used, which can also be downloaded from GitHub, for the correct
color display. For grabbers that are not listed, a separate LUT can be
created under HyperHDR as “lut_lin_tables.3d”, which is never used in
the case that PicCap/Hyperion is running on the TV. See image Flatbuffer
Log. It is always applied that “flat_lut_lin_tables.3d”. (edited)

Hi, you have to decide whether the LEDs should be RGBW NW or WW and
whether the material should be white or black, as well as the correct
length. In example picture, SK6812RGBW NW (natural white) in 5 M length
for ESP/WLED or WW for RP2040 HyperSerialPico as @awawa-dev recommends.
In addition, depending on the quantity of LEDs, you need the appropriate
power supply unit, for example for a 65-inch TV approx. 4.5 m strips
with 60 LEDs/m you need a power supply unit with 5Volt 75-100W output
power. (5Volt 14-20 Ampere at 100 % brightness) Not to neglect the
necessary 14 to 20A fuse, see https://wled-calculator.github.io/

Here is a website with a calculator https://wled-calculator.github.io/
Click in the dropdown option in the top of the page to change the
language from German to English to make it more readable, if you're not
german tongue. It'll also tell you what fuse you might want to solder
between the led strip and the power supply, i mean, you don't need it,
but if the led strip or PSU goes haywire, the fuse will blow itself if
more power is pulled than the led strip can handle, so you don't get a
fire or so the led strip may not die, and/or if PSU has no fuse
built-in, the PSU won't die. Fuses are dirt cheap, so you would do
yourself a disservice not using one. (edited)

Awawa made a guide here:
https://www.hyperhdr.eu/2023/02/ultimate-guide-on-how-to-build-led.html?m=1
I'd use HyperSerialPico and the Multi Segment wiring method, i'd also
use the 5v SK6812 RGB+CW led strip (CW means Cold White, but it's
actually neutral and closer to 6500k color temperature, the NW is warm
and WW is very warm), and use a 5v power supply that can deliver more
power than 100% of the led strip needs, i mean, sure, you're likely not
going to run the led strip at 100%, more like 50% or lower, cause 100%
is way too bright (and lifespan of your led strip goes down
significantly faster at 100%), but it's nice to have wiggle room so you
don't accidentically blow the power supply and it's fuse. (edited)
