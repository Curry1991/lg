.pclin 10/20/2022 9:28 PM
The more LEDs are used and the higher the frames/s are, the stronger a
flickering is perceived, which want to smooth out again, this leads to
delay. Stripes based on WS2812b should not be operated above 30 frames/s
due to their maximum possible speed.
[.pclin]
.pclin 10/20/2022 9:15 PM
ESP32 with LAN delay is less than 1ms, with Wlan it is normal 6ms but
with Wlan it can sometimes be up to 17 ms. ping is bidirectional, so you
can halve the times again. ping -c3 esp32-Wlan 64 bytes from
wled-ESP32.fritz.box (192.168.40.11): icmp_seq=1 ttl=255 time=5.95 ms 64
bytes from wled-ESP32.fritz.box (192.168.40.11): icmp_seq=2 ttl=255
time=5.85 ms 64 bytes from wled-ESP32.fritz.box (192.168.40.11):
icmp_seq=3 ttl=255 time=5.74 ms ping -c3 esp32-olimex-LAN 64 bytes from

64 bytes from ESP32-ETH-L.fritz.box (192.168.40.12): icmp_seq=2 ttl=255

[.pclin]
.pclin 10/20/2022 7:38 PM
144 LED/m produces mainly colored surfaces. 30 to 60 LED/m is more
detailed for Ambilight, and a large amount of LED e.g. 60 Hz requires
quite a bit of CPU power and RAM. ESP32 can calculate about 65k-85k LEDs
per second (that means 1000 LEDs @~70fps, 2000 LEDs @~35fps, 4000 LEDs
@~18fps) ESP8266 can calculate about 15k LEDs per second (that means
250LEDs @~60fps, 500 LEDs @~30fps, 1000 LEDs @~15fps) Arduino can
calculate significantly less LED than an ESP8266! ESP32 512kB, ESP8266
64 kB, Arduino 2kB (edited)
