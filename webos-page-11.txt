.kriilunaus 05/27/2025 2:38 PM
I think I have already described this in the past here in the channel -
it is not possible to say what will definitely break because it depends
on the model, which exact version you install and how the TV is
configured. I can only comment on what I have already observed and on
what may break. Updates/upgrades may require settings/databases to be
migrated from the TV's flash memory. In other words, data that is not
supplied as part of the firmware update files. These migrations are
typically one-way, from an older version to a newer one. This can then
lead to all kinds of errors after a downgrade: Lost or broken TV
settings, broken channel lists, non-functioning calibration data,
services hanging in a crash loop or overflowing log files causing a
failsafe trigger (TV reset) etc. From the user's perspective, this can
manifest itself in many different ways. For example, certain functions
may stop working, or the image quality may suddenly deteriorate. In
addition, webOS firmware updates regularly contain updates for chips /
modules (WiFi/Bluetooth, HDMI, Audio etc.) installed on the TV.
Downgrading does not automatically reverse these module firmware
updates. In the worst case scenario, this can lead to the affected
module / component no longer functioning at all because it is no longer
properly addressed by the system. Most of these problems can be solved
with the appropriate knowledge and tools, but if you don't have them,
you should keep your hands off a downgrade. Also, problems when
downgrading to the next smaller webOS minor or patch version are much
less likely than when switching to a completely different major version.
