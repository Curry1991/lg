mr.meorzipan 08/22/2024 9:21 PM
With Termux and SSH. Note, you download Termux from Github, Not the
Google Play Store, the one on Play Store is outdated and will not be
updated, according to the devs, Google's policies are too restricting,
therfore the devs recommend the Github version. You download the
developer mode app on your TV, it's literally called 'Developer Mode' in
the LG Content Store. You also have to register an LG developer account
on LG's site, cause when you open the app, you'll be prompted to login.
When you use the app, you first enable 'Dev Mode' (it's an option in the
app), this basically makes the TV run a SSH server. Then you enable the
Keyserver (that's also an option within the app), cause you need to get
the SSH key, this key is basically your password to connect to the SSH
server running on TV, this key can be obtained through that keyserver,
the keyserver is a webserver/website hosted on a port on your TV, when
you connect to webserver with your web browser and try to download the
SSH key file, you need to input a password, this password is given to
you in Developer Mode app after you turn on the keyserver. You need to
use the SSH key in your SSH client, for example one you run on your
phone. After successfully setting it up, you can connect to the SSH
server of the TV as the 'prisoner' user, and run whatever command you
want (can't do root privilege ones though), what you want to do is
download the IPK of Homebrew Channel onto the TV (can use curl command
for that, giving it the direct URL to the IPK for Homebrew Channnel on
Github) and install the IPK. Once Homebrew Channel is installed, you can
install or update YouTube Adfree through that. Reminder, as long as you
want to keep Homebrew Channel and whatever app and modification to not
be deleted, Dev Mode must Not be turned off in the Developer Mode app,
or else it deletes the 'prisoner' user, and you must be sure to keep an
eye on the timer, the Developer Mode app imposes a timer when you enable
Dev Mode. (edited)

generally very limited in this respect. In contrast to the consumer
rights against the retailer from whom you bought the TV and with whom

a product improperly or damages it by unusual means. In quite some
countries, a manufacturer does not have to offer a warranty for its
products at all; legal claims usually only arise against the retailer.
