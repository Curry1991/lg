satdx62 03/05/2026 8:25 PM
The SK6812RGBW CW/NW/WW 5-volt version has a power consumption of
approx. 48.67 W/9.73 A with a total of 200 LEDs and 100% brightness (RGB
white + white). The 12-volt version consumes only 35.87 W/2.99 A with
the same number of LEDs. If you decide on the 12-volt version, make sure
you purchase the correct LEDs, which are individually addressable and
not in groups of 3 LEDs. If you choose HyperSerial or HyperserialPico,
you have many correction options in terms of white balance/temperature.
The developer of HyperHDR & Co. recommends the CW version. I use both CW
and NW and was able to calibrate both really well.

[satdx62]
[] fpsusername Any difference between wled serial (s2) and hyperserial
(pico)?
satdx62 02/13/2026 7:12 PM
These are two different things. The HyperSerial WLED is a pure WLED
driver that has been enhanced with a fast USB transfer protocol (AWAWA).
HyperSerial/Pico, on the other hand, are standalone drivers that are
individually tailored to specific LEDs (RGB, SPI, RGBW, and seren
variants (CW, NW, and WW)). They are tuned so that the LEDs meet the
specifications.

satdx62 02/06/2026 11:22 AM
Since the cable length is very short, there can be no voltage drop
during cabling. However, the voltage drop increases with the number of
LEDs, as their consumption adds up and less than 5 volts can be measured
at the end of the strip. If there is only one power supply at the
beginning of the LED strip, homogeneous white illumination can no longer
be guaranteed above a certain length. For this reason, depending on the
number of LEDs used, several voltage/current injections are recommended.
And don't worry: the switching power supplies are very well stabilized
and can easily handle the connected LED load that does not exceed the
maximum tolerance. (edited)

RGBW LEDs are housed in an 8-pin chip package) and tested it
immediately. It is connected to a HyperSerial on an ESP32 S2 Mini. This
also applies to the RP2040 Pico and FTDI. Using the previous HyperHDR
version (22.0.0beta1) with the 'RGB Byte Order' setting in the LED
controller set to 'RGB', the colours were swapped, i.e. green was
displayed instead of red. After switching to GRB, the colours were
correct. Unfortunately, after restarting, the colours were swapped
again, as they were when I switched it off and on again. However, with
the new 'RGB Byte Order' FIX version that I sent you, everything works
as it should, even after a restart, with the 'RGB Byte Order' setting
set to GRB in the LED controller software. To provide a visual
comparison with the live image, I would need to mount it on a TV for
further testing. (edited)

within the illumination radius. With a darker color, it is difficult to
achieve a reasonable color balance. With 30 LEDs per meter, unsightly

effect. Therefore, 60 LEDs per meter are ideal here.

Hello, You do not need two separate segments for 240 LEDs. One segment
is sufficient for both HyperSerial/Pico and WLED in terms of response
time and latency. It is more important to use a suitable power supply
that provides sufficient output power and has at least two power feeds,
preferably three, to your LED strip. Please refer to
https://wled-calculator.github.io/ for more information. You should
definitely opt for 60 LEDs per meter and RGBW such as the SK6812. To
stay up to date, you should also follow the “piccap-hyperion” thread.

satdx62 12/24/2025 12:16 AM
All connection diagrams are based on 5-volt LEDs and a corresponding
5-volt power supply. However, since you have connected the SK6812 LEDs
in the 12-volt version 1:1 as shown in the diagrams, this cannot work. 5
volts must be applied to pins 4 and 14 of the SN74AHCT125N. Connection
to a 12-volt power supply is not permitted. Therefore, when using a
12-volt power supply, an additional 5-volt reducer circuit is required
to supply the level shifter with the correct voltage. However, if you
are using a 5-volt power supply, the 5-volt voltage is not sufficient to
light the 12-volt LEDs.
