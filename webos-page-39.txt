mr.meorzipan 07/09/2023 3:33 PM
Some very early models of the C9 do have panels that are capable of
120bfi (newer ones have panels on which will not work and probably
misbehave with 120bfi), however, you will have way more aggressive ABSL
or ABL (not sure) then CX and C1, and may not work as well as 120bfi on
LG CX and C1 (maybe cause of extra circuitry?). In general with bfi, you
will also want to disable VRR/Gsync, and keep a very stable framerate at
120hz or 60hz (depending on 60 bfi or 120hz bfi) to get the smoothest
and flicker free experience. On LG CX, with OLED Motion Pro, there are 3
levels, depending on the setting, you'll visually lose some brightness
(the screen is actually still as bright, but black frames visually
decreases brightness, so still try be careful with burn-in), but it'll
feel and look more closely like double the framerate. If you game on PC,
i'd also recommend looking at using a tool like Special K, you'll be
able to reduce latency to the lowest possible, using it's latent sync
option. You can implement HDR in SDR games, better than AutoHDR in
Windows (or using SpecialK, turn off the bad HDR in games and instead
use SK's HDR). If it's a D3D11 or game, you can remaster the render
pipeline from 8,10,11-bit to 16-bit, and eliminate banding in games. If
you have a D3D9 game and Nvidia GPU, can use the DXVK fork there to
upgrade games render pipelines to scRGB and 16-bit, and with vulkan
support in SK and Vulkan DXGI functionality in Nvidia's drivers, make
the game like D3D11, so you can get SK's HDR in there, windowed
fullscreen and lower latency with latent sync or VRR (with BFI you want
to use latent sync). In general, latent sync will give lower latency
than VRR, you will also be able to avoid the gamma shift on LG TV's that
may happen when VRR activates, where minor black detail may be lost.
