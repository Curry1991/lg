Hi, The RP2040 is better hardware than the ESP32. However,
HyperSerialPico from awawa-dev can only be flashed onto
RP2040-compatible boards. The comparable HyperSerial from awawa-dev, on
the other hand, can only be used effectively if the ESP32 boards used
also support the recommended speed. A distinction must be made between
special serial chips such as the ESP32 MH-ET Live (CP2104 or CH9102x
with 4 MB speed) and the ESP32-S2 Lolin mini (CDC with 5 MB speed). The
CP2104 or CH9102x requires a specially compiled WebOS driver, while the
ESP32-S2 Lolin mini is supported by WebOS without any additional
drivers. No special drivers are required to use RP2040-compatible boards
(only boards with built-in level converters are recommended here). In
addition, a distinction must be made between SPI (Serial Peripheral
Interface), a communication protocol, and “pixel LEDs,” i.e.,
addressable LEDs or LED groups (Neopixel LEDs) that can be controlled
individually. One should not be blinded by features that do not apply.
They are part of a chain that is connected to a controller via the SPI
protocol. Each pixel LED can set its color and brightness independently
of the others. Each pixel LED can set its color and brightness

If the power supply provides the required current (A), the voltage
should be a constant 5 volts at maximum brightness and white. However,
the difference between 5 volts and 3.5 volts is too high, meaning that
the LEDs can no longer be controlled correctly. The problem should be
solved by replacing the power supply. There have also been reports of
counterfeit power supplies that supposedly deliver a higher current. In
reality, however, they did not even manage to do that and broke down
after a short time under full load. In my opinion, a Mean Well LRS-100-5
Case 5 V/DC/0-18/90 W, which can handle up to 18 A, is the right choice.

Hi, Since both the FTDI and the RP2040 draw their operating voltage of 5
volts via USB, it is also possible to control 12-volt LEDs such as the
WS2815. However, due to the different operating voltages, a level
Shifter may be required between the DATA output of the LED controller
and the DATA input of the LEDs. The WS2815 has an additional DATA line
as a backup (BI) in case an LED in the chain fails. In practice, I have
not noticed any differences between FTDI and RP2040 when using the
SK6812RGBW LEDs with the correct HyperHDR settings. However, as the
WS2815 only has RGB and no separate white channel, I can't judge its
color reproduction and accuracy because I don't own it myself. Unlike
the RP2040, the FTDI controller does not require any additional
firmware. It is used as a passthrough. The FTDI, from your link, is
correct and can be used for this. In my tutorial, I described the use of
an FTDI controller with 5-volt LEDs. See:
https://github.com/satgit62/How-to-Install-and-Set-Up-Ambilight-on-LG-webOS#ftdi-ft232h-led-controller.
And
https://github.com/satgit62/How-to-Install-and-Set-Up-Ambilight-on-LG-webOS#controller-type-ftdi-ft232h.
When using 12 Volt LEDs, a LEVEL shifter may have to be connected in
between and a 12 Volt power supply for the number of your LEDs. See the
WLED power calculator: https://wled-calculator.github.io/ (edited)

[] fosiacat pico pi 2040 that much worse than an esp32 for this? i have

satdx62 07/28/2025 8:17 PM
The RP2040 Pico has better hardware than an ESP32 and is controlled
directly via the USB interface of the TV. Thanks to the ultra-fast DATA
transmission mode of the “Adalight” driver for HyperSerialPico, there is
always interference-free data transmission between HyperHDR/Hyperion.NG

[] aesthetiqaly Also probably a dumb question but do I need to order

satdx62 07/05/2025 9:35 AM
The controller is of course compatible with SK6812RGBW. If you want to
achieve the best possible results, you should use SK6812RGBW CW with 60
LEDs per meter instead of WS2812B for your setup. The LEDs are supplied
with a connection adapter that can be plugged directly onto the LED
strip. The other side with the three wires is connected directly to the
output of the controller. As the voltage decreases at 200 to 300 LEDs,
you must install an additional power supply at the end of the LED strip.
The additional current of 5 volts (pay attention to + and -) is taken
from the same output of the controller. To do this, use two AWG 18
silicone wires, which you either solder at the end of the strip or
connect to the LED strip using a 3-pin LED adapter with 10 mm when you
cut it to size. Alternatively, you can use WAGO terminals. The
recommended accessories are shown in my instructions in the hardware
section. See:

Hi, This solution is suitable for all users who are looking for a very
simple solution. FTDI-FT232H LED controller If you are looking for a
simple solution, you should take a look at this controller. It requires
no firmware and no level shifter and can control both RGB and RGBW LEDs.
The connection to the LEDs is also very simple. For SK6812 RGBW and
WS2812B LEDs, connect the AD1 output of the FTDI to the DATA input of
the LEDs and the GND output to the -5V GND input of the LEDs. For APA102
(SPI) LEDs, connect the AD1 output of the FTDI to the data input of the
LED and the AD0 output to the clock input of the LEDs. Also connect the
GND output of the FTDI to the -5V GND input of the LEDs. The controller
is supplied with power from the TV set via USB. HyperHDR/Hyperion.NG and
FTDI communicate losslessly via the USB port of the TV with a very high
baud rate of 3200000, so that a network connection is not required. See:
https://github.com/satgit62/How-to-Install-and-set-up-Ambilight-on-LG-webOS#controller-type-ftdi-ft232h
and

You can use any standard 10 mm 3 PIN LED connector. (I don't know this
one, and I can't see it clearly when enlarged). Better call it the
original link. It is difficult to draw a white wire on a white
background. Therefore, the GND (-5 volts = white) is drawn in black in
the diagram. The red wire stands for +5 volts. The +5 volts and the -5
volts from the power supply can be connected to the fuse and the LEDs
using WAGO terminals, which are resealable terminals. You can also use
the German version, which looks slightly different. The LEDs are always
supplied with a coupling adapter that is used to connect the controller
to the LEDs. The green DATA wire is connected to the DA screw of the
controller, the white wire of the LEDs is connected to GND (-) of the
controller. The red wire is not used, as the controller draws its power
via USB. I intentionally drew the diagram in a childish way so that
everyone can understand it and make the connections. When you are ready
and want to put the whole thing into operation, I can give you further
instructions.

The RP2040 with HyperSerialPico is the reliable LED controller when it
comes to connecting/transmitting data and displaying LED colors. The
high-speed serial controller achieves a baud rate of 5,000,000 via the
USB port of the LG, so that there are no transmission errors or
time-outs. You should also consider the ESP32 S2 Mini (CDC: 5Mb speed)
with HyperSerial. Like the RP2040, it is connected via the USB port and
does not require a special kernel driver on the LG. The ESP32 MH-ET Live
(CP2104 or CH9102x: 4 MBit/s) also works with HyperSerial, but the
appropriate kernel driver must be installed. You should therefore make
sure that there is a driver for your own device.

ready-made controllers where you don't have to solder or flash anything

Skydimo is surprisingly decent. For anyone looking for a relatively
simple solution, it works shockingly well, so long as your monitor isn't
directly up against your wall. A few notes: 1. Install the appropriate
driver before buying anything, just to be sure it will work. The main
guide has a link to drivers needed for devices like this. If you can
load the driver without an error message, you're probably good to go. 2.
Get the 32" version. This is the best choice for our 42"~48" LG oleds.

Consider USB "power enhancement" cables. I daisy chained two of them,
connecting to all 3 ports my C2 had, and I haven't (yet) had issues
driving the Skydimo strip. Way brighter than I expected at 100% white,
although I limited mine to 75% brightness to be safe. In theory these
ports are 900mA max each, and that's if we're lucky and they all are
intended to support an external HDD. 4. My hardware-related settings are
attached, and they're good enough for me. The refresh cap on these seems
to be 48hz (in Smoothing).

satdx62 05/06/2025 12:57 PM
The Plasma 2040 can be used to control 2 LED segments. Output is: CL
(first segment) and DA (second segment) connection. You can connect the
power supply of the board to the 5 V pin, but it makes no sense as the
control follows via USB. Since Plasma 2040 can draw up to a maximum of 3
A via USB-C, this is only enough to operate a limited number of LEDs
(max 60 LEDs). So, above a certain number, the LEDs absolutely need
their own power supply. See LED Power
Calculator:https://wled-calculator.github.io/
