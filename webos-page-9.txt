I post this for information and educational purposes only. I had this
exact issue on the older of my 2 LG TVs (2016). I went into the In-Start
menu and the Wi-Fi module was listed as N/A (not available). I took the
back off the TV, disconnected the cable from the Wi-Fi module and the
other end from the main board (while I was in there I also disconnected
and reconnected all other cables, just for the sake of it!). When I put
it all back together, Wi-Fi was and still is working fine to-date. No
more delay in finding the access point or Wi-Fi no longer being
available on the TV. After a number of years (or humid environments,
like outdoors!!!) the connections can oxidise and create no or
intermittent connection issues. Removing and replacing them effectively
breaks the oxidisation and "cleans" them. To be certain, do it a few
times (carefully!!!) to be sure. If you are not confident to remove the
back and do this yourself, get someone with knowledge and experience to
do it for you. There are high voltages still present on certain
components (capacitors) on the power supply board for some time after
disconnecting power, so do not touch any components on the power supply!
Again, DO NOT REMOVE THE BACK OF THE TV AND POKE AROUND IN THERE IF YOU
LACK THE EXPERIENCE AND/OR KNOWLEDGE TO DO SO SAFELY!!! I accept no
responsibility for anyone ignoring this disclaimer and injuring
themselves...or worse! It is also possible that your Wi-Fi module has
actually failed, in which case replacement is the only option. (edited)
