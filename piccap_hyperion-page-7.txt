Hi, Smoothing takes place in HyperHDR after the data has been received
from PicCap. Only then is the processed data forwarded to the LED
controller. This process is very computationally intensive and depends
on the performance of the device on which HyperHDR is running. I think
it's worth running HyperHDR on the TV as well, as communication takes
place in real time directly on the TV, thus avoiding bottlenecks caused
by Wi-Fi in your network. Ideally, HyperSerial/Pico should be used as
the LED controller via a direct USB connection instead of WLED via
Wi-Fi. If you prefer WLED, I recommend an LED controller with a LAN
connection.

    curl http://192.168.x.x:8090/json-rpc?request=%7B%22command%22:%22componentstate%22,%22componentstate%22:%7B%22component%22:%22ALL%22,%22state%22:false%7D%7D

No additional splitters or grabbers are required to use Ambilight on an
LG TV with “root”. The HDMI sources are analyzed by
PicCap/Hyperion-WebOS. Depending on the setting, both video and UI are
recorded/captured and forwarded to HyperHDR/Hyperion.NG. This can be
seen in the live preview window or in the LED visualization of
HyperHDR/Hyperion. However, if you do not receive a preview, there may
be various reasons for this, such as incorrect settings in PicCap.
Depending on the webOS version (old or new), different
libraries/backends are required for UI and video capturing
(Hyperion-webOS). Even within the same webOS version, the libraries for
UI and video capturing differ. Since automatic detection does not always
work, the correct backend version must be forced for older webOS
versions. Some TV models are compatible with a specific backend, but
require a slightly modified routine to work reliably. This is where the
quirks come into play. The resolution you choose is also very important.
You can also find out how to set up your Ambilight in my
guide:https://github.com/satgit62/How-to-Install-and-set-up-Ambilight-on-LG-webOS

saturation, gamma, etc. HyperHDR also has countless static and dynamic
background effects that can be turned on and off on the fly. These can

[satdx62]
[] ziyadovich Basically, I do not need to calibrate if I install lut
compressed and uncompressed
satdx62 06/26/2025 3:46 PM
You must install either the compressed or the uncompressed version, but
not both. Compressed LUTs save flash memory, but hyperHDR needs a few
milliseconds more in real time to decompress them. This bothers some
people, which is why they prefer the uncompressed ones. Personally, I
don't mind this, which is why I only use the compressed ones, as the
flash memory is more important to me. Self-calibration is only required
in rare cases for DV material played via HDMI from certain devices and
video format configurations.

satdx62 06/24/2025 9:19 AM
Hi, HyperSerial or HyperSerialPico is controlled via the serial
interface of the RP2040 or ESP32. In our case, this is done via the USB
port, whereby the WLAN or Bluetooth interfaces, if available, are not
used. The RP2040 Pico is currently the most powerful hardware for
HyperSerial and has the additional advantage that it is recognized by
WebOS without the need to install a dedicated kernel driver. It is also
available with an integrated level shifter, eliminating the need for
additional tinkering. To be able to use ESP32 with HyperSerial, you need
ESPs that have a specific serial chip and meet the minimum speed
requirements. These are the ESPs with serial/USB chip CH340G and
CH9102x. However, a kernel driver must be installed on LG for this,
which is not available for all new devices and must be compiled by the
user. The ESP32 S2 Mini Lolin is an exception. It is recognized by
WebOS, does not require a kernel driver and meets the requirements. A
level shifter must also be installed for all ESPs. ESP chips with the
serial/USB chip CP2102, on the other hand, do not fulfill these
conditions and are not compatible. I am not aware of any HyperSerial
firmware for the Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W. The only Raspberry Pi with WLAN
that runs with HyperSerialPico is the Pimoroni Plasma Stick 2040 W.
However, the firmware for this is also not suitable for WLAN control. If
you do not want to do without WLED gimmicks, you can use HyperSerialWLED
with a maximum speed of 2 Mb. (ESP32 incl. S2, S3, C3). Here the
Adalight USB serial protocol with 115200 baud is replaced by the AWA
protocol with data integrity check and 2000000 baud. Finally, I would
like to point out that HyperHDR can be fully controlled and automated
via the JSON API interface, including static or animated background
effects, regardless of the LED controller used. You can therefore exert
influence via your network (LAN/WLAN). (edited)

satdx62 06/12/2025 11:22 AM
I don't know whether the LEDs are switched off at 85% dark gray or more,
because the LUT table contains a very fine gradation of color tones.
Even in very dark scenes, a bluish or greenish tone is therefore
displayed, although it is perceived as dark gray on the TV screen. If
you look at the preview window in HyperHDR with the Developer magnifier/
Color pipette, you will also see that the color is not grey. Only @Awawa
can provide concrete information on whether a possibility exists or
could be implemented. Here is an excerpt from the HyperHDR instructions
from
https://www.hyperhdr.eu/2021/04/how-to-set-up-hyperhdr-part-i-basic.html
"The most important options are: 3 gammas, ‘Backlight threshold’ and
‘Colored backlight’. Red/Green/Blue gamma' determines the intensity/gain
of the individual color channels. The backlight threshold is minimal
gray backlit of your led strip so it won't turn OFF causing flickering.
Colored backlight" tries to preserve a part of original color to set
backlight instead of gray. You will probably want to have the backlight
enabled as the ‘Smoothing/Antiflicker filter’ works best when ‘Colored
backlight’ is enabled and the ‘Backlight threshold’ is set to 2-3,
depending on your LED configuration and needs." (edited)

A beta version is always a pre-release version of the final version. The
v21.0.0.0 is the final version. It supports the compressed LUTs. You
can't just compress as you imagine. It will not work. You need the ZSTD
compression algorithm and need to follow my guide to compress the LUT's
in the correct format. You can also use uncompressed LUT's. To your
question about calibration. A LUT (Look-Up-Table) is a type of pre-made
color correction. Similar to a colour filter, it is applied to a video
to adjust or optimize its colors. In the calibration process, the color
spectrum of specially created videos, such as SDR, HDR and Dolby Vision,
is analyzed. Using a special algorithm, the necessary corrections are
made and saved in a LUT table. Each of these three types of content has
its own table with its own name. When you play something on your device,
HyperHDR immediately recognizes the content and loads the corresponding
LUT to apply the appropriate color algorithms to the LEDs. This is just
a simplified explanation. (edited)
